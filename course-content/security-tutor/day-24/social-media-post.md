# Day 24 — CEH Checkpoint #3: Closing the Loop on Old Mistakes

## LinkedIn

Three checkpoints in a row, I got the same nmap question wrong: what's the difference between a "closed" port and a "filtered" port?

Today, on attempt three, I finally got it clean — and in the same session, closed out three other mistakes I'd been carrying for weeks.

Day 24 was a pure checkpoint session — no new material, just a 10-question quiz pulling together everything from the last few days (authentication vs. authorization, vertical vs. horizontal privilege escalation, and the three flavors of credential attacks: brute force, credential stuffing, and password spraying) plus a running list of every concept I'd gotten wrong or fuzzy on before.

The result: all four of my standing "watch items" got resolved in one sitting. The nmap port-state confusion (RST = closed, silence = filtered — I'd had it backwards three times). SameSite cookie mechanics — this time I got the actual *reasoning* right (top-level navigation vs. embedded request), not just the memorized outcome. A concurrency model from two weeks ago that I retained without any re-teaching. And a privilege-escalation labeling slip that turned out to be a genuine one-off once tested against a fresh scenario.

The real lesson from today isn't the security content — it's how you actually confirm a mistake is fixed. Re-asking the same question just tests memory. Testing the same *concept* with a brand-new scenario tests understanding. That difference is the whole point of a checkpoint.
